# The weekly model against baselines that can actually fight back

Generated by `npm run baselines:weekly-prob`, offline from
`reports/2026-08-20/brier-prospective.json.gz` — 3,573 player-weeks of the
2025 season, fingerprint `bba1d103`.
Every figure is leave-one-week-out on identical rows.

## Why this exists

The shipped `brierSkillScore` is measured against **climatology** — always
forecasting the position's base rate. That is the weakest baseline available, and
it is not a fair fight: the model is handed a Sleeper projection and the constant
is not. The question a professional reader asks is what the model adds over the
projection it was given.

The last column is the one that matters. The binned baseline uses the same single
input, learned non-parametrically out of fold — it is what the model has to beat
to justify being a model rather than a lookup table.

## Discrimination: the model cannot reorder anybody

| position | AUC, logistic | AUC, raw projection | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| QB | 0.6181 | 0.6294 | -0.0113 |
| RB | 0.7654 | 0.7701 | -0.0047 |
| WR | 0.7476 | 0.7497 | -0.0021 |
| TE | 0.7395 | 0.7435 | -0.0040 |

**This is structural, not empirical.** A one-variable logistic is monotone in its
input, so it cannot change the ORDER of players relative to the projection. Its AUC
is the projection's AUC by construction, and the table confirms the implementation
matches the algebra.

So the model's contribution is **calibration, not discrimination**. It does not tell
you who is more likely to clear the line than the projection already did — it tells
you *how* likely, on a scale that has been measured. That is a real and useful
thing, and it is a smaller claim than a Brier skill score against a constant
implies.

## What a reader should take from this

- The model is **well calibrated** and that is genuinely worth having: a stated 70%
  happens about 70% of the time, which a raw projection does not tell you.
- It adds **no ranking information** over the projection. Anyone choosing between
  two players at the same position can read the projections and get the same order.
- Its advantage over a **binned lookup of the same projection** is the honest
  measure of what the parametric form buys, and it is far smaller than the
  headline number.
- **QB remains the weak case** on every baseline, which is why `hasUsefulSkill`
  puts it below the floor.
