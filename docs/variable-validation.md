## The question, and why it is not "does X correlate with scoring"

Targets correlate with next-half scoring strongly, and almost all of that is *good
players get targets and also score*. A correlation reported without controlling for
points-to-date is a statement about who is good, not about what to do differently —
and a variable shipped on that basis adds a column repeating what the first column
already said. Every headline figure below is a **partial** Spearman, controlling for
first-half points per game.

## The forest: can a non-linear model beat points-to-date?

Every model in this product is linear. A random forest can represent interactions and
non-monotone effects that a linear fit cannot, so it is the fair test of whether the
linear tools were leaving anything on the table. Both figures are **out-of-bag** — a
forest's training-set R² is near 1 by construction and means nothing.

## What this cannot tell you

- **A first-half/second-half split is not a draft-time test.** Everything here
  observes nine weeks of the current season. Protocols 1–3 tested the pre-season
  regime and it failed; nothing in this document revives it.
- **Leave-one-season-out is not a frozen holdout.** All eight seasons were visible
  while these variables were chosen. A survivor here is a candidate for a frozen
  protocol, not a validated signal.
- **Survivorship.** Requiring six observed and four scored games conditions on staying
  healthy and rostered, which is itself predictable. The effect is toward
  understating variance, not toward inventing correlation.
- **The forest is a screen, not a product.** An uncalibrated model must not be
  presented as production-safe, and nothing here ships.
